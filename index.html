A story of resourcefulness

From a Dead Mouse to Riches

The Jataka tale illustrates how a person can recognise opportunity, begin with very little, add value and reinvest thoughtfully.

💭 The merchant's challenge

A poor young man overhears a rich merchant tell the king: “Even a dead mouse can be the start of a fortune, if someone works hard and uses their brain.” The young man takes the words seriously and begins looking for an opportunity.

01 · Spot opportunityHe sells a dead mouse to a shopkeeper for two coins to feed the shopkeeper's cat.

Principle: Curiosity and market awareness.

02 · Start with capitalHe uses the coins to buy fruits and water, then trades with tired flower pickers.

Principle: Risk-taking and resourcefulness.

03 · Add valueHe sells the flowers in the city market and reinvests the returns.

Principle: Combining resources and value addition.

04 · Expand wiselyHe clears storm-fallen branches for firewood, trades grass, opens a refreshment shop and builds trade relationships.

Principle: Operational flexibility and expansion.

05 · Give backHe becomes wealthy, shares half his fortune with the merchant who inspired him and supports the community.

Principle: Gratitude, ethics and social responsibility.

Business language

Your startup glossary

Understand these essential terms and see how they appear in everyday business.

TermMeaningBusiness example
ProfitMoney remaining after paying all costs of producing and selling goods or services. Total Revenue − Total Expenses.₹500 sales − ₹300 costs = ₹200 profit.
Fixed CostExpenses that remain constant despite short-term changes in production or sales.Monthly shop rent, equipment insurance.
Variable CostExpenses that change with the volume of goods or services produced or sold.Flour, sugar, lemons, per-unit delivery.
InvestmentMoney or resources put into an asset, venture or project with the expectation of future income, profit or value.Buying a bakery oven.
InventoryGoods, materials and raw assets held for production or resale.Flour bags, unsold bread, packaging.
AccountingRecording, tracking, organising and reporting business money to assess financial performance.Daily cash register or monthly P&L account.
ChawlA traditional multi-storey residential tenement with dense, single-room units, historically found in Mumbai.The document describes the early Lijjat Papad setting as a Mumbai chawl.
MortgageA loan secured against an asset used as collateral.The document describes M. S. Oberoi mortgaging his wife's jewellery to acquire a hotel.

Fixed costs

For the bakery example, monthly rent of ₹40,000 remains the same whether 10 cakes or 1,000 cakes are produced in the short term.

Variable costs

As cake orders increase, the bakery needs more ingredients such as flour, sugar and eggs, so these costs rise with production.

Learning from Indian enterprise

Small beginnings. Remarkable journeys.

The document's case studies show how opportunity, quality, learning, trust and persistence can shape an enterprise.

🥙

Shri Mahila Griha Udyog – Lijjat Papad

Beginning (1959): Seven homemakers in a Mumbai chawl began with borrowed equipment, a traditional recipe and ₹80 starting capital.

Working model: Members rolled papads at home and returned finished products. Consistent quality was essential.

Ownership: Registered as a cooperative society in 1962, with equal ownership and profit sharing among members.

Growth and impact: The document reports expansion into other products and exports, and cites 2023 figures of over 45,000 women members, 83 branches and ₹1,600 crore annual turnover.

Quality • Cooperation • Women’s empowerment
🏨

Rai Bahadur Mohan Singh Oberoi

Early life: Born in Punjab in 1898; moved to Shimla and worked as a hotel desk clerk earning ₹50 a month.

Learning: Gained experience in guest relations, billing, housekeeping and administration.

Risk: In 1934, mortgaged his wife's jewellery and invested personal savings to acquire a struggling Shimla hotel.

Legacy: Built the Oberoi Group through customer satisfaction, employee training and high standards. The document notes the Padma Bhushan in 2001.

Learning • Calculated risk • Service quality
🎨

The Dandekar Brothers – Camlin

Beginning (1931): Digambar and Govind Dandekar began a small stationery business in South Mumbai, selling liquid ink in recycled glass bottles.

Market opportunity: They saw that Indian artists lacked access to quality art supplies.

Innovation: They travelled to Glasgow to study colour chemistry and later established a laboratory to develop products suited to Indian needs.

Growth: Expanded into pencils, crayons, geometry boxes and paints.

Market gap • Research • Product innovation
🌏

The Nattukottai Chettiars

Roots: A traditional entrepreneurial community from Tamil Nadu, known as merchants, bankers and financiers.

Trade networks: Operated across India, Sri Lanka, Burma (Myanmar), Malaysia and Southeast Asia in salt, rice, textiles and finance.

Success principles: Business ethics, trust-based lending, careful accounting and reinvestment in community temples, education and welfare.

Trust • Record-keeping • Community

Your startup blueprint

Eight questions. One business plan.

A business plan is a written blueprint that sets out business goals and the steps to achieve them. Use these eight considerations to shape an idea.

01

Needs and Idea

What problem is being solved? Example: fresh, affordable whole-wheat snacks in a neighbourhood with limited options.

02

Potential Customers

Who will use the product or service? Consider children, young adults, families or other groups.

03

Money and Costs

What initial investment is needed for equipment, rent, materials and salaries? Where might funds come from?

04

Location

Where will the business operate: near schools or markets, or through an online delivery model?

05

People and Skills

What roles and skills are needed? For a bakery: head baker, kitchen helpers, cashier and delivery staff.

06

Pricing and Profits

What price covers costs and allows a profit while remaining affordable?

07

Promotion and Marketing

How will customers discover the business? Consider social media, posters, opening offers and packaging.

08

Challenges and Risks

What could go wrong? Consider perishable inventory, rising ingredient prices and local competition.

How a business works

The four functional areas

⚙️

Management & Operations

Daily processes, quality assurance and workflow. Tasks include purchasing equipment, sourcing ingredients, checking freshness and managing supply chains.

💰

Finance & Accounting

Financial health, budgeting and transaction tracking. Tasks include recording revenue and expenses, cash-flow management, P&L statements, taxes and loans.

🤝

Human Resources (HR)

People, skills and workplace culture. Tasks include recruitment, training, employee benefits and resolving conflicts.

📣

Marketing & Sales

Customer awareness, brand building and revenue. Tasks include packaging, promotion, discounts and customer service.

Worked example

Total Revenue = ₹1,20,000 + ₹75,000 + ₹85,000 + ₹15,000 = ₹2,95,000.

Total Expenses = ₹2,22,000.

PBT = ₹2,95,000 − ₹2,22,000 = ₹73,000.

If tax is ₹13,000, PAT = ₹73,000 − ₹13,000 = ₹60,000.

Common mistake: subtract tax from PBT, not directly from total revenue. PBT/PAT are presented as in the supplied learning material.

Enterprise and India

Startups and nation building

Entrepreneurship can create jobs, solve local problems, encourage innovation and contribute to a more self-reliant and developed India.

🇮🇳

India's startup ecosystem

The source document describes India as the world's third-largest startup ecosystem and cites around 120 unicorns in 2025. A unicorn is a startup valued at over US$1 billion.

Major hubs named: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Mumbai and Delhi NCR.

Technology sectors: E-commerce, fintech, health-tech and agri-tech.

🧩

National initiatives and vision

Startup India: A flagship initiative described in the source as supporting young businesses through regulatory support, incentives and funding access.

Atmanirbhar Bharat: A self-reliance vision focused on domestic manufacturing, local innovation and reducing import dependence.

Viksit Bharat 2047: The vision of a developed India by the centenary of independence.

👩‍💼 Job creation

Enterprises can create employment and support household incomes.

🏭 Self-reliance

Local innovation can help meet needs and reduce dependence on imports.

💚 Social responsibility

Businesses can support schools, hospitals, communities and environmental initiatives.

Failure can be a teacher

Not every startup succeeds immediately. An honest attempt that does not work can reveal gaps in customer demand, operations or financial planning. Learning from setbacks can help entrepreneurs build stronger ventures.
